### ☁️ 1. **Cloud (Public Cloud)**

This means everything runs on the internet using a cloud provider like AWS, Azure, or Google Cloud.

- You don’t own the hardware.
- The provider manages servers, storage, and networking.
- You access everything online.

**Example:**  
Using Google Drive or hosting a website on AWS.

✅ **Pros:** Easy to set up, scalable, low upfront cost  
❌ **Cons:** Less control, ongoing costs, needs internet

---

### 🖥️ 2. **On-Premises (On-Prem)**

This means everything is hosted locally on your own physical servers (like in your office or data center).

- You buy and maintain your own hardware.
- Full control over systems and data.

**Example:**  
A company running its own servers in a server room.

✅ **Pros:** Full control, better for sensitive data  
❌ **Cons:** Expensive, requires maintenance, harder to scale

---

### 🔀 3. **Hybrid**

This is a mix of both cloud and on-premises.

- Some resources stay on local servers.
- Others run in the cloud.
- They are connected and work together.

**Example:**  
A company stores sensitive data on-prem but uses the cloud for backups or web apps.

✅ **Pros:** Flexibility, balance of control and scalability  
❌ **Cons:** More complex to manage

---

### 🧠 Quick Summary

- **Cloud:** Everything online
- **On-Premises:** Everything local
- **Hybrid:** A combination of both
